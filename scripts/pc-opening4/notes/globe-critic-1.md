# Globe critic, round 1 (2026-11-27) — verdict: FAIL, score 3/10

Judged images: review/globe/a-*.jpg, b-*.jpg, sheets, animatic.mp4.
Severity 1 = must fix, 2 = should fix, 3 = polish. Numeric targets are 8-bit values after the view transform unless stated.

## Issue 1 — /home/user/real-estate-agent-Yuuri/scripts/pc-opening4/review/globe/a-3.2s.jpg (also a-crops.jpg crop 1, b-3.8s.jpg, b-4.4s.jpg; every frame) (severity 1)

**What:** MOST DAMAGING, and the single change that would help most. Every cloud is a 2D cut-out. In the stills the satellite cloud image is thresholded onto a flat shell: binary edges, one flat salmon value per cloud, dot-matrix speckle fields, no thickness, no shading on the tops, and no readable cast shadows on sea or land although the sun is only 1-7 deg high (shadows should be 8-30 times the cloud height). b-3.8s has a featureless flat sheet across the whole upper left. The cloud sea in b-4.4s is a waxy low-frequency blob surface with dimples, in a colder white than the salmon layer beside it. Cloud covers 40-70% of every frame, so this is what makes it read as a game globe, and it also hides Japan and the Alps.

**Fix:** Build one cloud system for orbit and dive. (1) No threshold: continuous optical thickness from image luminance minus the clear-sky surface (full 8-bit range, gamma about 1.5), then erode the edges with fine 3D noise (0.3-5 km) so 4K 1:1 edges are wispy. (2) Give it height: top = 1.5 km + up to 8 km scaled by thickness (or from a cloud-top temperature layer of the same pass), base about 1 km. (3) Light it per frame with the same sun as the ground: march the cloud height field towards the sun for self-shadow and for shadows cast on land and sea (length = height / tan(sun elevation); a 5 km top at 5 deg throws 57 km), shade the tops by slope, let thin parts show the ground, fill shadowed cloud with blue sky light. (4) Choose a day (or edit the field) with cloud mostly over the seas and under 20% over Honshu's land and the Alps. (5) Cloud sea only within about 80 km of Hotaka and only below about 60 km camera altitude, top at 1,600-2,000 m with 0.5-2 km billows, same sun colour as every other cloud.

## Issue 2 — /home/user/real-estate-agent-Yuuri/scripts/pc-opening4/review/globe/a-3.2s.jpg (also a-crops.jpg crops 2-3, a-1.8s.jpg, b-3.8s.jpg, b-4.4s.jpg) (severity 1)

**What:** The picture is monochrome salmon-mauve; real dawn from orbit is a blue/orange picture. Measured in the stills: sea grey-violet (97,97,111) to brown (95,76,74), night sea purple (30,24,30), land a cold teal-grey (64,75,91), haze under the limb mauve (148,131,144), clouds one salmon-tan (168,142,128) even far on the day side. In the current build (b stills, animatic) lit land is salmon like desert rock (195,170,158) and everything in shadow is one flat navy (77,88,107). There is no blue-black sea, no green-brown land, no white cloud.

**Fix:** (1) Add sky fill (a multiple-scattering term, or a blue ambient of roughly 15-25% of the direct light at 5 deg sun) to the in-scatter and to surface light so haze and shadows turn blue (hue 210-225), and check that ozone absorption is not tinting the in-scatter purple. (2) Sea: albedo 0.03-0.06, colour from Fresnel-reflected sky plus deep-water blue; away from glint aim near (20-45, 40-70, 80-120) by day and blue-black at night; red must never exceed blue. (3) Undo the global desaturation of land: lit forest about (70-95, 65-80, 40-55). (4) Clouds: peach-gold (hue 28-38) only on sun-facing parts where the sun is under 8 deg, neutral white (R-B under 6%) where it is above 12 deg, blue-grey shadow sides. (5) Grade after AgX: move warm highlights 10-25 deg of hue towards yellow and lift their saturation about 1.2x so sunrise reads gold, not pink.

## Issue 3 — /home/user/real-estate-agent-Yuuri/scripts/pc-opening4/review/globe/a-0.5s.jpg (also a-1.8s.jpg, a-sheet.jpg, animatic f0-f56) (severity 1)

**Fix:** Previz first at 640 px with coastline + lights only, no cloud. Hold at under 1% approach per frame for 1.5 s, then arc left around the Alps (camera ground track SE -> E -> NNE) to heading 200 by 4.4 s: about 10 deg/s for the first 2 s, peak under 45 deg/s, under 5 deg/s after 4.4 s. The far limb is then marked by a thin airglow line and stars until the dawn limb swings in from the left. Alternative to compare: start from the north-west on heading about 140 (sunrise and glint in frame, only a 60 deg turn, but Japan inverted). Fallback if the arc feels too wild: keep heading 200 but start at about 2,400 km so the whole archipelago is in frame. In every case keep cloud off the land for the first 2.5 s and separate sea from land at night (faint blue sheen on water, black land). Gate: a fresh viewer shown frame 24 for one second says 'Japan'.

## Issue 6 — /home/user/real-estate-agent-Yuuri/scripts/pc-opening4/review/globe/a-0.5s.jpg (also a-1.8s.jpg) (severity 1)

**What:** City lights are flat beige plateaus with hard edges that fill the plains like painted sand. Peak value is only about 206/255 and the 90th percentile of lit pixels is 191, so Tokyo's core is no brighter than its suburbs. No fine structure (roads, separate towns), no hot cores, no glow through haze; clouds cut sharp holes in the lights instead of diffusing them; Korea is a solid orange patch with a ruler-straight coastal edge; lights are still at full strength where dawn light has already arrived (1.8 s). Night clouds are neutral grey and sharper than the lights, and the faint blue night glow of the brief is missing.

**Fix:** Rebuild the emission from radiance, not from the toned picture: subtract the night map's base tint, apply a steep curve (gamma about 2-2.5) and scale so only city cores clip and suburbs sit 3-5 stops lower; warm-white cores, amber outskirts. In comp add a two-radius glow (tight: about 0.15% of frame width; wide: about 1.5% at about 5% of the energy). Under cloud multiply the lights by exp(-thickness) and blur them by cloud thickness, so cloud over a city glows amber. Tie one auto-exposure to the sunlit fraction of the frame so lights sink as the frame brightens and vanish only where direct sun has landed. Night side: cloud at most 4-6% display luminance, soft-edged, tinted deep blue.
