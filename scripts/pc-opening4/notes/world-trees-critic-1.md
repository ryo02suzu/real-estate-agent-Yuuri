## Issue 1 — r1-c2-trees.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** SINGLE MOST USEFUL CHANGE. Turning the trees on darkens all ground by 3 to 5 stops, including ground with no tree on it. The sky and the far snow peak are identical in the two c2 frames (ratio 1.000), so exposure is the same. Yet the bare sunlit slope (x1180-1270, y380-440) falls from (129,96,53) to (32,22,11), 0.068 of its linear brightness (-3.9 stops), and its scree chutes and pale streaks are replaced by flat tan. Rock beside the camera, far above the tree line (x1020-1270, y700-720), falls to 0.11; a sunlit rock facet (x345-400, y330-370) to 0.14; the river bed (x620-690, y620-700) to 0.058, so the braided river no longer shows; a shaded snow slope (x0-50, y370-470) from 20 to 1.1. Of the ground (rows 250 to 719), 80% is now below 4 against 33% without trees, and its mean falls from 18.3 to 4.7. The frame without trees is the better picture. The reveal shows the same signature: three shaded slope boxes each lose 4.7 to 4.8 stops (factor 0.035 to 0.038) when trees are on. A loss this uniform on bare and forested ground alike points to the forest-floor layer (the one change to the terrain material in the note) acting on all ground inside its 4096x3840 image, not only under forest.

**Fix:** Make the floor layer follow the stems that were actually planted. Mix factor = planted stems per 8 m cell divided by the full-density count, blurred 12 to 16 m, clamped to 0-1; it must be 0 wherever no stem stands within 16 m, and 0 on river gravel, scree, snow and everything above the tree line. Floor colour = half litter and dwarf-bamboo tone (linear 0.05 to 0.09) and half the photo blurred to 16-24 m, so the photo's patchwork survives; never under 0.04. I did not read the code, so check two things first: that the mix factor really is the image's alpha (alpha can be dropped or premultiplied on save), and that the floor image is read as linear data, not sRGB (0.074 read as sRGB becomes 0.0065, 3.5 stops darker). Checks: (1) a colour-only pair (emit=1) with trees_floor=0 and 1 at 640x360: every pixel that changes by more than 5% lies within 16 m of a planted stem. (2) Re-render c2 with trees at 1280x720 and divide linear means with and without trees: bare slope box 0.9 to 1.1 (now 0.068), camera-side rock 0.9 to 1.1 (now 0.11), rock facet 0.9 to 1.1 (now 0.14), river bed at least 0.8 (now 0.058), snow slope at least 0.8 (now 0.038).

## Issue 2 — r1-reveal-trees-full.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** In the hero frame the forest is a black cut-out. Below the horizon 208,718 pixels are under 8 and only 740 are lit crown (0.35%); 22.5% of the whole frame is under 4. The near-left slope (x150-350, y560-680) reads 0.7 with a median of 0; the same pixels without trees read 12.4 (median 9.9). The cloud deck beside it is (43,70,94), so the forest sits 7.7 to 8.7 stops under the cloud by mean. A conifer canopy reflects 3 to 5% of light and a cloud top about 70%; under the same sky, forest belongs roughly 4.5 to 5.5 stops under the cloud. A mountain photographer would say at once that ridges above a bright cloud sea are never ink-black inside. The ridge 1 to 2 km away and the one 8 km away are both 0 to 2, so all ranges merge and depth is lost (no haze is visible in these frames either). In c2 the shaded forest (x50-400, y480-680) reads 0.3 against 7.7 and has lost the blue sky light: pixels of blue hue are 23.6% of lit ground without trees and 0.5% with them. In sun, the pixels where the photo itself shows forest (under 55 without trees) read 19.8 against 47.9. The aerial photo already records how bright a forest canopy is from above; planted trees should add grain, shadow and outline, not take away 2 to 5 stops.

**Fix:** Hold every tree render to one rule, measured on a with/without pair as linear means: sunlit forest seen from above 0.5 to 0.9 (now 0.23); shaded or backlit forest 0.4 to 0.8 (now 0.035 to 0.038); ground without trees 0.9 to 1.1 (issue 1). In the reveal EXR the forest slope should be 4.5 to 5.5 stops under the shaded cloud deck (now 7.7 or more). Steps: (1) Issue 1. (2) Stand test, about a minute on 4 CPUs: 200 m x 200 m of flat ground with the floor material, fir LOD3 at 470 per hectare, world = plain white at strength 1, no sun, Standard view, 256x256, 64 samples. Reflectance = mean pixel value: 0.03 to 0.05 from straight above and at least 0.03 from 20 degrees above the horizon. Tune the base colour of the LOD foliage until it holds (expect a facet albedo near (0.05, 0.10, 0.045)), then set trees_gain to 1. If the test reads under 0.01, look for normals facing inward with a back-face branch, a per-tree attribute (tint or gain) that reads 0 on one level, or too much weight on the see-through part of the material. (3) Close the canopy (issue 3) so the sky-lit crown tops fill the view instead of the floor. (4) With the world builder: the fill and haze of world critic issues 5, 6 and 11 must land too, because in today's grade a correct forest would still read only 4 to 8. With the cloud's shadow side at 90 to 130 the forest should land at about 10 to 22 before haze and 18 to 32 with it, darkest 1% at 6 or more, spread inside a 100x100 px patch at least 4 levels, and the 8 km ridge at least 10 levels lighter and bluer than the nearest ridge.

## Issue 3 — r1-skyline-crops.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** The distant fir is a pencil and the stand is open, which is the wheat-field look the builder reported. On the library's own sheet (lod-match.jpg) the LOD3 fir, variant 0, is 14.0% as wide as it is tall, against 19.3% for LOD1 of the same tree; LOD3 larch is 10.1% against 23.8%. LOD3 was matched to LOD1 by covered area, and a solid shape that covers the same area as a see-through tree must be narrower than it. A 17 m fir is then 2.4 m wide: at 470 trees per hectare that covers 21% of the ground (42% for a 24 m tree), where a closed fir stand covers 70 to 90% with crowns about 4.6 m across. In the crops the lit trees are dashes 2 px wide and 4 px tall (58% are 2 px wide or less); brightness holds together for 1.0 to 1.1 px sideways but 2.4 to 2.7 px vertically; the texture carries 5 to 8.5 times more energy as vertical stripes than across them. In c2 about two thirds of the lit "forest" is ground colour (mean green-to-red ratio 0.79, between ground at 0.70 and crown at 0.96); a closed forest seen from 20 degrees above shows no ground at all.

**Fix:** Match LOD2 and LOD3 to the outline of LOD0, not to its area. Fir crown width (95th percentile of the width profile) 25 to 33% of height, widest at 20 to 35% of the height; make 25 to 30% of stems a broader hemlock type, 35 to 45% wide with a rounded top. Recover the brightness match with holes (hashed transparency of 35 to 50% on the skirts, or tiers built from 7 to 9 separate ragged fans) and with albedo, never by narrowing. Where far trees are thinned and enlarged, widen them by 1 over the square root of the kept share but raise height by 15% at most. Checks: a top view (orthographic) of a 100 m x 100 m LOD3 stand at 470 per hectare is 70 to 90% covered; the same stand from 20 degrees above the horizon at least 95%; in a 3840-scale crop the stripe ratio is 2.5 or less and sideways correlation at least 2 px; in c2 the ground-coloured share inside closed forest is under 10%.

## Issue 4 — r1-c2-trees.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** The fir is khaki and gold, not dark green, and forest no longer differs from open ground. On the library sheet the fir crown in the film sun has a hue of 52 to 55 degrees at every level (LOD3 mean (173,166,115), saturation 0.42). In c2 the planted slope (x700-960, y440-560) has a median hue of 42; across the frame 76% of lit ground pixels lie at 25 to 45 degrees and 2% between 65 and 160. Sunlit fir needles under a 3,300 K sun belong near 75. Rim light in the reveal crops has hue 39 and saturation 0.40, the colour of larch, on a forest that is 98.4% fir. The photo holds forest and bare ground 2.4 stops apart (47.9 against 110.1); with trees they are 0.7 stops apart (19.8 against 27.7), so the dark-forest and pale-ground patchwork that makes a mountainside read as real is flattened. LOD3 larch is lemon yellow, (237,219,110), hue 52, value 0.97, and c2 contains no saturated orange pixel at all (hue 18-42, saturation over 0.6, value over 0.35: 0 pixels), so the golden larch is nowhere on screen.

**Fix:** Calibrate LOD2 and LOD3 colour against the needles only of LOD0 (mask out trunk and branches) under plain white light, then confirm in the film sun: fir crown mean hue 65 to 85, saturation 0.30 to 0.45, and in sky-lit shade blue not below red. In c2, forest 1.8 to 2.6 stops below the bare sunlit ground beside it. Rim light on fir: hue 50 to 65, saturation 0.35 or less, confined to the outer 15% of the crown width; in a one-tree backlit test the middle half of the crown is at most 15% as bright as the rim (make the see-through share depend on the edge, facing under 0.25, and give skirts thickness so light must cross two surfaces mid-crown). Larch LOD3: hue 35 to 42, saturation 0.55 to 0.70, value 0.80 or less, width at least 20% of height. Pass to the tree-library owner: the LOD0/1 fir needs the same shift at source, with darker and bluer needles and foliage dense enough to hide the trunk in the upper two thirds.

## Issue 5 — r1-reveal-trees-full.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** A uniform carpet: one species, one height class, even spacing. Skyline teeth are evenly spaced on both ridges I measured: spacing varies by 0.39 of its mean on the near ridge and again at 8 km, where a random pattern of the same density gives about 0.55 with this measure. Along 270 px of near ridge edge (x250-520) there is no gap reaching the ground and no tree standing clear of its neighbours: teeth come every 3.5 px, 81% of the gaps are 2 to 4 px, median depth is 1.3 px, and the deepest is 6.5 px, at most half a tree height. 98.4% of stems are fir; broadleaf, birch and dwarf pine are left as flat photo. A forester would say plantation. The Ministry of the Environment page the builder cites names fir forest and Erman's birch forest together for 1,500 to 2,500 m; in November the birch stands as pale grey crowns in gullies and near the tree line, between gaps, dead tops and fallen stems. Placement does follow the photo (see praise), but that mosaic is invisible in the black reveal and flattened in c2.

**Fix:** (a) Bare broadleaf: where the summer photo is bright green below the tree line, plant leafless birch and other broadleaf at 250 to 400 stems per hectare. A cheap far model is enough to start: a rounded see-through crown 8 to 16 m tall (hashed transparency 70 to 80%, twig colour about 0.09, trunk 0.35 for birch). Target 15 to 35% of stems between 1,600 and 2,400 m, concentrated in gullies (concave ground, slope 30 to 45 degrees) and in the top 150 m below the tree line. (b) Gaps: clear stems from 6 to 10% of forest area in patches 15 to 40 m across (noise of 60 to 120 m wavelength) and move 60% of the existing dead trees to their edges. (c) Height: a per-tree factor with a spread of 25% (0.5 to 1.4), 3% of trees at 1.25 to 1.4, and saplings of 2 to 6 m for 20% of stems at gaps and edges. Checks on a skyline about 2 km away: spacing variation 0.55 or more; per 400 m at least 3 notches that drop 60% of the local tree height and are 8 m wide, and at least 5 trees standing 5 m above their neighbours.

## Issue 7 — r1-reveal-trees-full.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** Lit crowns are single-pixel sparks, which will twinkle in motion. In the bottom-left corner (x0-140, y650-720) 2.06% of pixels are isolated bright points (over 60 and over 4 times their 5x5 median) on a median of 1.0; the brightest is 211, above the sky at the horizon (204). The nearest trees read as strings of fairy lights on black, with no modelling of the crown. At 3840 scale the lit bands keep almost as much sideways contrast at a 2 px period as at 8 px (power falls only about 3 to 5 times), so every tree is its own 1 px streak. A real lens and the overlap of real crowns would blend this into a soft warm fringe. Version 3 already needed a re-render for forest flicker.

**Fix:** On LOD2 and LOD3 foliage set roughness to 0.6 or more and specular to 0.02 or less (a needle glint cannot be sampled at this size). Clamp indirect light to 3-5. Render tree frames at 128 to 192 samples with a noise threshold of 0.01 to 0.02 and the default 1.5 px Blackman-Harris filter. Add lens softness in the comp before grain (Gaussian 0.6 to 0.8 px at 3840). Checks: isolated bright pixels under 0.05% of tree pixels; two renders of frame 600 with different seeds differ inside the tree area by at most 6 levels at the 99th percentile; then frames 594 to 605 at 1280 played as a loop show no twinkling points.

## Issue 9 — r1-skyline-crops.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** Edges. (a) With the floor colour running over scree, gravel, snow and ground above the tree line (issue 1), forest and open ground have no edge in tone, and the river bed has lost its braids. (b) The tree line thins out gradually, which is right, but it ends on flat tan: the dwarf pine and birch scrub that the photo shows as dark green above the last firs is erased, not planted. (c) Where forest meets cloud, crop A shows a ruled line: straight runs up to 89 px (about 200 m), a transition 2 px wide, and a third of the roughness of the skyline above it (0.53 against 1.66 px), so along the whole shore no tree top stands out of the cloud. That is the sheet cloud's doing, but it is where the eye checks the trees.

**Fix:** (a) After issue 1, add a ragged belt 10 to 25 m wide at every forest edge: saplings and half-height trees, density falling to zero across the belt with 20 m noise; no stems on gravel bars, on scree brighter than 0.25, or within 15 m of water. (b) Dwarf pine as a low dark cover 0.5 to 1.5 m high (albedo 0.04 to 0.05, hue 90 to 110) where the photo is dark green between 2,400 and 2,700 m on slopes under 35 degrees; birch scrub 3 to 6 m tall in the top 150 m below the tree line. (c) Re-test with the volume cloud: keep trees planted down to 150 m below the deck (already so), let cloud density fade over 40 to 120 m from the slope (world critic issue 9), and at 3840 scale require a forest-to-cloud transition 15 px or wider, tree tops of the first two rows visible through mist, and no straight run over 30 px. Polish worth trying once that works: a thin mist 60 to 100 m deep on the lee side of the nearest crest, so the sun draws shafts between the crowns.

## Issue 10 — r1-c2-trees.jpg (severity 3)

**What:** Season and larch. The film's sun (azimuth 115 degrees, 5 degrees high) is where the real sun stands on about 8 to 9 November at 36.25 N; in late November it would be near 121 degrees. That is useful: Weathernews (27 October 2024) says the Kappa-bashi colours normally peak from mid October and, in that late year, lasted "into early November", with golden larch named as the attraction. So gold larch at 1,500 m can be believed for 8 November and not for the 27th. Either way the pictures carry no larch: 5,670 of 350,701 trees in the reveal, all near the cloud line, and 69,057 in c2 in a valley that renders black.

**Fix:** Call the date 8 November and keep the sun. Larch golden (hue 35 to 42) only below about 1,600 m; from 1,600 to 2,300 m plant it bare or with under 30% of its needles, as rust-brown twigs, on rocky ribs, avalanche-track margins and river terraces, 3 to 8% of stems. In c2, once issue 2 restores the sky light, shaded gold larch on the valley floor should read 25 to 45 at hue 35 to 45, and any stand that catches the sun 110 to 170. If gold is wanted in the reveal, the honest source is larch under the cloud seen through gaps, not an orange rim on fir. ## Praise (keep these)

(1) Scale is right. Skyline teeth (median 2.1 px, largest 6.3 px at 3840) are consistent with 17 to 20 m trees at the 8 km the geometry gives. (2) Skylines are serrated out to about 12 km: roughness 1.45 px against 0.14 px on the bare ridge behind. The smooth dune edges of the last round are gone inside the tree range, and the warm rim now comes from crowns, not from a cream band of ground; at 1280 it reads as a thin gold line. (3) Placement follows the real photo: tree texture and photo brightness correlate at -0.67 on the sunlit c2 slope, steep ribs stay bare, and the upper edge thins out gradually. Density (470 per hectare) and heights (24 m at 1,400 m down to 2.4 m at 2,520 m) come from cited field data. (4) No grid and no repeated tree can be found: the strongest narrow peak in the horizontal spectrum is 1.5 to 2.3 times its neighbours, 143 mesh types are in use, and everything is hashed from world position, so nothing changes between frames. (5) Cost is sound: 350,701 trees add 29 s to a 103 s frame at 1280 and peak at 5.1 GB. (6) The with/without pairs are clean (sky and far peaks identical to three digits), which is what made this review measurable; keep delivering pairs. (7) The builder's own list of weaknesses is accurate on blackness, pencil firs, the missing broadleaf and the 15 km limit. The single change that helps most is issue 1: stop the forest-floor layer from repainting ground that has no trees. It alone gives back 3 to 4 stops on every bare slope, river bed and rock face in c2, restores the real photo that was the best thing in the last review, and it has to be right before any tree colour or brightness can be judged.
