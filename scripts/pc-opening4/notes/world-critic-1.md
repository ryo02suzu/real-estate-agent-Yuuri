## Issue 2 — reveal-volume.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** SINGLE MOST USEFUL CHANGE. The cloud sea is a flat table covered in equal-sized, steep-sided puffs, each about 40 to 60 m across (8 to 12 px at 640 px, 2.5 to 4.5 km from the camera). That is cotton wool, roughly ten times too small for a cloud sea, and it is why the scene reads as a tabletop model. There is no kilometre-scale relief, so a 5 degree sun has nothing to draw long shadows and warm faces on, and beyond about 8 km the deck becomes a smooth white strip. A real cloud sea over a basin is calm stratus: long swells whose crest lines stack up toward the horizon. Those lines are the main depth cue.

**Fix:** Reshape the cloud-top height field. Add swells of 2 to 5 km wavelength with +/-60 to 100 m height, and a second band of 0.6 to 1.2 km with +/-20 to 30 m. Enlarge the present cell pattern 3 to 4 times (to 150 to 250 m) and cut its height to +/-10 to 15 m at most; keep only a faint 30 to 60 m wisp layer at +/-3 to 5 m. Stretch everything about 2.5:1 so crest lines run roughly across the view (along the basin's north-south axis). Mean top stays at 1,650 m. Fade density to zero over the top 30 to 50 m. Check: crest lines stay visible as light and dark stripes to at least 30 km, and swell shadows are several hundred metres long.

## Issue 3 — reveal-volume.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** The cloud visibly ends. Down a pixel column the deck reads 226 where the volume stops (row 185 of 360, about 13.5 km), then drops to 184 on the sheet behind it (a 42-level step, about 0.65 stop) and stays a darker beige-grey band up to a hard horizon step (210 against a 247 sky). It reads as: the cloud stops and a grey plain or sea begins. The sheet-only version (reveal-surface.jpg) is worse: a blue plastic sheet (101,131,158) two stops darker than the sky above it (247,237,224), meeting it in a one-pixel line.

**Fix:** One cloud system all the way to the horizon, which is 87 km away for a camera 590 m above the deck. Drop the surface sheet in every shot that looks across the deck. Use nested rings like the terrain: 0 to 14 km as now with full detail; 14 to 40 km the swells and the 0.6 to 1.2 km band only, with coarser sampling; 40 km to the horizon a plain untextured volume slab 150 to 200 m thick whose top is displaced by the same swells. Same density, colour and phase in all three; cross-fade neighbouring rings over 2 to 3 km. Check with a column profile: no step above 4 levels (8-bit) between 5 km and the horizon, and the deck gets brighter, not darker, toward the horizon.

## Issue 4 — reveal-volume.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** The cloud does not respond to backlight. Tops are an even white (mean 206, most pixels between 151 and 231), as if lit from overhead; shadows on the cloud are neutral grey (110,106,103), not blue; the deck under the sun is no brighter than elsewhere and no swell has a bright rim. A real cloud sea facing a 5 degree sun shows warm sunlit faces, cool blue-violet shadows many times longer than the swells are high, and a glow on the deck beneath the sun.

**Fix:** Cloud volume: strong forward scattering. Henyey-Greenstein anisotropy about 0.8, or the Volume Scatter node's Mie phase with droplet diameter 10 to 20 micrometres if this Blender offers it (the Blender manual describes Mie as for cloud and fog). Scatter colour 0.98 to 1.0, no absorption. Body density for a mean free path of 25 to 50 m. Volume bounces at least 12 on GPU. Do not whiten the sun: at 5 degrees it is about 3,300 K, roughly (1.0, 0.62, 0.32) linear; shadows must be filled by a blue sky dome. Targets after the view transform: sunlit swell faces within 15 degrees of the sun at 215 to 245 with R minus B at least 35; shadowed troughs at 90 to 130 with B minus R at least 25; deck under the sun brighter than deck at the frame edges. Optional if the plan allows: lowering the sun from 5 to 3 degrees doubles shadow length.

## Issue 5 — reveal-volume.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** Washed-out exposure. Sky runs from (213,213,214) at the top to (253,246,236) at the horizon, so there is no blue at all; 4.8% of the frame is above 245; the darkest 1% of pixels is 42 and the minimum 31, so nothing is black. Backlit ridges sit at 57 to 59 like sunlit sand. It looks like a fogged, overexposed frame, not a photograph taken into the sun. The sky of reveal-surface.jpg is just as flat: 198 at the top, 239 at the horizon, about half a stop of range.

**Fix:** Lower exposure about 0.8 to 1.2 stop from the reveal-volume setting and add contrast (a medium-high contrast look). Judge by targets, not by the number: top of frame 150 to 175 with B minus R at least 45; horizon at the frame edges 195 to 215 with R minus B at least 45; only the 2 to 3 degrees around the sun may clip; near ridges in shadow at a mean of 18 to 32 with the darkest 1% at 6 to 12. In the Sky Texture set Altitude to the camera height (2,240 m) if the option exists, and Aerosols to about 0.2 to 0.4. A search summary of the Blender 5.0 manual gives Aerosols 1 as city air; the page itself was blocked from here, so confirm it in the manual. If sky=0.5 dims only the dome and not the sun, return it to 1.0 and use exposure instead. Do this in the view transform and grade, never by clamping the render.

## Issue 6 — reveal-surface.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** No air. Yatsugatake and the Southern Alps, 60 to 135 km away, are near-black cut-outs (14 to 20), darker than the ridge 1 km from the camera (35 to 41), with full-contrast snow speckle, standing on a ruler-straight horizon where brightness drops 61 to 83 levels in one pixel row. The sun is a hard-edged 14 px white disc: sky is 226 right beside it and 216 at 25 degrees away, so there is no glow and it reads as a daytime moon. reveal-volume.jpg has haze and its far ranges fade about right (roughly a quarter of their contrast left at 68 km), but the haze is uniform, so it whitens the whole frame instead of layering it.

**Fix:** Two haze layers, always on, each a plain untextured slab so it stays cheap. (1) Clear air from the cloud top up to about 6 km: extinction 0.010 to 0.013 per km, bluish scatter colour about (0.6, 0.78, 1.0). (2) A moist layer in the first 400 m above the cloud top: about 0.04 per km in the lower 200 m and 0.02 above, neutral-warm, anisotropy about 0.8. Targets: contrast left against the sky behind is 75 to 80% at 20 km, 35 to 45% at 68 km (Yatsugatake), 12 to 20% at 135 km (Fuji); range bases dissolve into the moist layer so the peaks float; no horizon line. Sun: sky 2 degrees from the sun at least 2 stops above sky at 25 degrees, fading smoothly to nothing by about 15 degrees, from the forward-scattering layer or a depth-weighted comp element. Keep the sun unclamped in the EXR so the later lens glare has real values.

## Issue 7 — reveal-surface.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** Near ridges read as moss-covered sand dunes at toy scale. Three causes that trees alone will not hide: (a) the aerial photo is enlarged far past its resolution (0.48 m per texel at zoom 18 is 1:1 only beyond 1.2 km in a 2560-wide frame), so forest turns into lichen-like blotches; (b) skylines are perfectly smooth curves from the 4 m mesh; (c) every crest carries an even cream band (193,169,135; 5 to 12 px wide), the sunlit sliver of the far slope rendered as flat pale ground, which looks like a painted sand path. The dark backlit slopes themselves are correct; do not add fill light.

**Fix:** Prepare the plate for the trees. Within 1.5 km of the camera path blur the photo to 8 m or more and use it only as a colour tint; give ground under forest an albedo of 0.04 to 0.08 with a detail material (litter, sasa dwarf bamboo, rock, light snow patches). Place trees from the photo (dark green = fir and hemlock, pale = bare birch or rock, none above 45 to 50 degrees of slope) and tint each tree from the photo pixel at its root. Put trees and dwarf pine on every visible crest out to 6 km at 1280 px (10 km at 2560) so skylines are serrated; farther out add a 5 to 10 m canopy bump to the terrain normals. Trees must cast shadows (11 times their height at 5 degrees) so the cream band breaks into streaks; the rim light should come from crowns, not from the ground. Add 0.3 to 2 m rock and ground displacement within 500 m.

## Issue 8 — reveal-surface.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** The aerial photo still carries its own midday lighting and is not calibrated as surface colour. A navy patch (3,20,35) with a clear outline sits in the middle of the right-middle ridge between neighbours at (5,23,24) and (18,31,30): a baked shadow or a mismatch between photo flights. In c2-photo.jpg sunlit bare slopes reach 232 while the sky is 172 to 208, and when the shadows are lifted the dark areas break into teal and purple blotches (compression colour noise amplified by the grade). The mid-left island ridge has a grey patch smeared down the slope.

**Fix:** De-light and calibrate the photo before it becomes base colour. Divide it by a hillshade of the same terrain lit from the photo's sun (roughly south, 50 to 60 degrees high) or by a 150 to 300 m low-pass of its own brightness, limited to +/-1 stop. Pull the blue cast of shadow pixels to neutral, and desaturate pixels darker than 0.05 by about 60% before the seasonal grade. Scale so forest lands at 0.03 to 0.06, dry grass 0.12 to 0.20, bare rock and scree 0.15 to 0.30, snow 0.8 to 0.9 (linear reflectance). On faces steeper than 45 degrees fade the photo out in favour of a triplanar rock texture. Re-check that the navy patch is gone.

## Issue 9 — reveal-volume.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** Where cloud meets slope there is a clean waterline: knife-sharp in reveal-surface.jpg, still one definite edge in reveal-volume.jpg. The deck reads as a lake of milk or a snowfield against rock. A real cloud sea laps the slopes with torn, feathered edges, tongues of fog reaching up the gullies and thin gaps on the lee side.

**Fix:** Modulate cloud density by distance to the terrain: within 40 to 120 m of the slope (noise-varied) fade density to zero raggedly, and add thin fog tongues at 20 to 40% of deck density rising 60 to 100 m above the deck in gullies on the shaded, camera-facing slopes. Raise the local deck top by 20 to 40 m where it piles against the ridge the camera has just crossed. No straight or smooth contact line longer than about 300 m anywhere in frame.

## Issue 10 — reveal-surface.jpg (severity 2)

**What:** The plate is not yet set up for what comes next, and the far ranges carry little weight. Logo: mid sky is 212 to 226, so a white logo has no contrast, and nearly half the frame is featureless sky. Swans: the deck is 206 to 223 everywhere, leaving no headroom for rim-lit white wings. Grade: the sky gradient changes one 8-bit level every 4 px on average and up to 13 px at 1280, so it will band once encoded. Scale: Yatsugatake stands only 21 px above the horizon at 1280 (about 1 degree, 3% of frame height). That is physically right for a 35 mm lens, but small.

**Fix:** Deliver a 32-bit linear EXR with the sun unclamped, plus depth and mattes for sky, cloud, terrain and trees. Logo zone (upper centre) at 185 or below and free of structure. Swans: light them with this sky plus the bright cloud deck as fill from below, lay haze over them by depth, and keep the deck they cross at 200 or below. Add fine grain (about 1.5 levels at 8-bit) before any encode. Framing: try 45 to 50 mm at the reveal (far ranges 1.3 to 1.4 times larger) with the horizon kept at 45 to 50% of the height. At 2560 use a 64 m far ring only inside the view cone, and render far snow as a soft tone, not single white pixels. Before the full sequence, render 12 consecutive frames at proof size to check the cloud volume does not flicker.

## Issue 11 — c2-photo.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** c2, judged separately, tone and season: there is no air in the valley and the colour is summer. 26.6% of the frame is below 16 and 12.4% below 8 (near-left slopes average 20, valley floor 21), blue-black and about 6 stops under the sunlit slopes. At 4 to 6 km even clean mountain air puts 5 to 7% of horizon brightness in front of a black object, so nothing that far away can be darker than about 50. Sunlit slopes are lime green (160,142,89), which is July, not November; snow shows as chalk streaks along gullies. The sheet cloud has the same faults as in the reveal: a blue sheet with hard shorelines and a razor-edged shadow at right.

**Fix:** Add the same clear-air haze as in the reveal (0.010 to 0.016 per km, denser lower down) plus a thin blue morning haze in the valley below 2,000 m. Restore the physical sun-to-sky ratio: sunlit and shadowed parts of the same material about 3 to 3.5 stops apart. Targets: shadowed slopes within 1 km at 20 to 35 with the darkest 5% at 8 or more; valley floor at 40 to 60 and bluish; under 2% of pixels below 8. Season: above 2,300 m turn bright greens to straw and umber (hue from about 45 to 30-35 degrees, saturation minus 30%; about (170,140,100) in sun) while dark dwarf-pine greens stay. Place snow by slope under 40 degrees, hollows and north or east aspect above 2,500 m, with soft edges, not by brightness. Use the volume cloud deck here too.

## Issue 12 — c2-photo.jpg (severity 1)

**What:** c2, judged separately, as a base: the photo-draped terrain is a sound base from about 1.5 km outward. Real landforms, the real braided river and the real forest mosaic make it the most convincing thing in this review (about 5/10 as it stands). It is not a base for close ridge shots. Within about 600 m of a camera at 3,050 m the slopes look like smooth folded cloth: the elevation data (5 to 10 m at best) has no crags, the photo is enlarged up to 12 times at 100 m (one pixel of a 2560-wide frame covers 4 cm there), and on steep faces the texture is smeared down the fall line (the lit facets of the left-centre peak).

**Fix:** Inside 600 m replace, do not enhance. Rock displacement of 3 to 12 m at 10 to 60 m wavelength plus 0.3 to 2 m at 2 to 10 m on slopes over 35 degrees. Scanned rock and cliff pieces (Poly Haven) along the visible skyline within 300 m. Scree scatter of 0.3 to 2 m stones on 30 to 38 degree slopes. Triplanar rock texture (2 to 5 cm per texel within 150 m, 20 to 50 cm out to 600 m) replacing the photo on faces over 45 degrees, with the photo blurred to 10 to 20 m kept only as tint. Dwarf-pine mats 0.5 to 1.5 m high and dry grass tufts on gentle ground below 2,900 m; fresh snow with thickness on ledges; a few thin cloud wisps (50 to 300 m long) drifting over the col at camera level for parallax. Keep 80 to 100 m clearance from any surface without hero geometry. From 600 m to 1.5 km a detail bump layer is enough.

## Praise (keep these)

Geography and astronomy hold up when measured from the frame: camera pitch about -2.0 degrees, cloud horizon at -0.80 degrees (-0.78 expected for 590 m above the deck on a curved Earth), Akadake at +0.21 degrees (0.25 expected), peaks within 0 to 3 px of their marks at 1280 (better than the 5 to 10 px the builder reported), Fuji correctly just left of Kaikomagatake, sun disc near true size, horizon level. No steps, no z-fighting and no tile seams in the terrain; skylines are clean. reveal-volume.jpg proves the right direction: warm haze, far ranges that fade with distance, ridge shadows falling toward the camera across the cloud. In c2-photo.jpg the real landforms, braided river and cross-lit relief are well beyond the old clay look. The single change that would help most is issue 2: give the cloud sea kilometre-scale swells and shrink the cotton-ball cells. It lets the 5 degree sun draw long cool shadows and warm faces, it supplies the receding crest lines that make the view read as tens of kilometres deep, and it makes the far deck cheap to carry to the horizon.
